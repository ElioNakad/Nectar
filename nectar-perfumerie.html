The Pour

Harvested at the hour honey turns to amber.

We work at the edge of an orchard where almond trees have kept the same bees for five generations. Nectar is drawn at dusk, when the last light thickens the honey and the ylang ylang gives up its heaviest oil.

What's left after distillation is not sweet so much as warm — a scent that behaves like skin does, changing with the hour and the person wearing it.

How To Wear

Three rules, no rubbing.

Prime

Cleansed skin

Apply after moisturizer, never before. Hydrated skin holds oil-based notes far longer than dry skin does.

Place

Pulse points

Wrists, neck, behind the ears, inner elbows — anywhere blood runs close to the surface and warms the oil.

Hold

Let it dry

Never rub the wrists together. Friction breaks the molecular structure and burns off the top notes early.
